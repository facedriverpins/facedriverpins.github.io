A floating drive pin is not defined by its position or diameter range, but by its dynamic behavior. Unlike fixed pins, each floating pin moves independently – typically 0.5 to 1.5 mm axially. This independent movement is enabled by internal wedge‑lever systems, hydraulic balancing, or spring mechanisms. When the tailstock advances, each pin retracts at its own rate until all pins contact the workpiece end face simultaneously. The result: automatic self‑leveling that compensates for face taper up to 5–7 degrees and irregularities as large as 3 mm.

Floating pins take repeated shock loads – interrupted cuts, hard spots, and variable engagement. Carbide would crack; ordinary alloy steel would deform. We choose S7 tool steel for all our floating pins. At 54–58 HRC, S7 offers exceptional impact toughness, excellent hardenability, and practical wear resistance. The result: floating pins that survive on crankshafts, camshafts, and heavy forgings without breaking.

Floating drive pins never work alone. They operate together with the face driver’s center point. The center point establishes the precise rotational axis, while the floating pins transmit torque. This “double‑center” system ensures that even on imperfect faces, every pin shares the load evenly – no single pin overworks, no slippage occurs.
