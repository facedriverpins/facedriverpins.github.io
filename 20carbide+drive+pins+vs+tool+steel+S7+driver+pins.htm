Drive pins are the heart of any face driver. They bite into the workpiece end face, transmit torque, and determine the reliability of your machining process. Choosing the right pin material is not a minor detail – it directly affects productivity, tool life, and part quality.

As a specialized manufacturer, we produce both S7 tool steel drive pins and carbide drive pins, each designed for distinctly different applications. Understanding their differences is the key to making the right choice.

S7 tool steel and carbide sit at opposite ends of the material spectrum. S7 is a shock‑resistant chromium‑molybdenum steel known for its exceptional toughness. Carbide (tungsten carbide with a cobalt binder) is a powder‑metallurgy ceramic that offers extreme hardness but very low fracture resistance.

S7 Tool Steel – Working hardness 54–58 HRC. Impact toughness is outstanding, roughly three times that of A2 or D2 tool steel. When overloaded, S7 pins wear gradually or deform plastically. They do not shatter.

Carbide – Hardness 88–92 HRA (approximately 70–75 HRC). Wear resistance is superb, often lasting 5–10 times longer than S7 in continuous cutting. However, carbide is brittle. A single heavy impact or an uneven end face can cause sudden chipping or complete fracture.
