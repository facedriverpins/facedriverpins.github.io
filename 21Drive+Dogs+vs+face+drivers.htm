Unlike conventional spring‑loaded or mechanical pins, power‑operated pins are actively controlled by an external power source – typically hydraulic or pneumatic pressure from the machine tool. The sequence is fundamentally different:

workholding Spring‑type pins – The workpiece is pushed against fixed pins by tailstock force. Clamping and driving happen simultaneously. Power‑operated pins – The workpiece is first positioned by a fixed center. Then, hydraulic or pneumatic actuators drive the pins forward into the end face. Clamping and driving are separate, controlled steps.

This active engagement delivers three major advantages: repeatable clamping force, higher precision, and the ability to machine parts with irregular end faces without risking slippage.

In the world of end‑face driving, most pins are passive. They rely entirely on tailstock force to push the workpiece against them. That works for many jobs, but what happens when you need higher precision, heavier torque, or consistent clamping force on every part? That is where Power‑Operated Drive Pins change the game – and when combined with a Full Width layout, you get the most advanced drive pin system available.

A Full Width layout means the pins are distributed evenly across the driver’s nominal working diameter range. This symmetric arrangement maximizes contact area and provides the highest possible torque transmission – typically 3,000 to 7,800 pounds of driving force.
