nlike a fixed center point that remains rigidly in place, a movable center point is designed to retract axially during the clamping sequence. The process is simple but effective: The tailstock advances the workpiece until it first contacts the movable center point. As tailstock force increases, the center point slides backward into the driver body, allowing the workpiece end face to meet the surrounding drive pins.

Movable face driver pins vs. Fixed Center Point drive pins

Feature Movable Center Point Fixed Center Point
Reference point On the workpiece face On the fixed center
TIR (run‑out) 0.015 – 0.02 mm typical 0.001 – 0.005 mm
End‑face requirement Low – compensates uneven faces High – needs precise center hole
Best for General turning, small batches, hard turning Ultra‑precision grinding, fine finishing

Once the drive pins are fully engaged and the workpiece is seated, the center point locks into its final position, establishing a constant reference plane on the workpiece face rather than on the center point itself. This sequence guarantees that every drive pin shares the load evenly, compensating for end‑face tilt up to 5–7 degrees and eliminating the need for a perfectly pre‑machined center hole.
