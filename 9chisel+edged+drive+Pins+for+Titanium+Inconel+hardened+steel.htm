Chisel‑edged pins are often called “double‑chisel” or “spade” pins. They are the preferred solution for the most demanding turning operations: heavy roughing, interrupted cuts, and difficult‑to‑machine alloys.

Standard pins work well on clean, uniform end faces. But real‑world workpieces are rarely perfect. Consider these scenarios:

Rough castings or forgings – Scale, sand inclusions, and uneven surfaces prevent standard pins from seating properly. A chisel edge cuts through the crust and bites into sound metal.

Interrupted cuts – Splines, keyways, cross‑holes – any break in the cut creates shock loads that can pop standard pins loose. The positive lock of a chisel edge resists this dislodging force.

Hard‑to‑machine materials – Inconel, Waspaloy, titanium, and hardened steel above 50 HRC are notoriously difficult to drive. A chisel edge creates a stress‑concentrated groove that effectively transfers torque even when the material resists indentation.

Extreme length‑to‑diameter ratios – When turning long, slender shafts, any slippage at the drive end causes chatter and poor finish. Chisel‑edged pins provide the unwavering grip needed for stable machining.

Unlike conventional pins that indent the workpiece, a chisel‑edged pin features a sharp cutting edge ground into its tip – exactly like a woodworking chisel or planer blade. Under tailstock pressure, this edge does not simply press into the metal; it machines a small groove into the end face, creating a positive mechanical lock. The result is significantly deeper engagement and vastly higher torque transmission than any standard pin can provide.

Like all drive pins, chisel‑edged pins are not standalone components. They work in close coordination with the face driver’s center point. The center point provides precise axial location and establishes the rotation axis. Once the workpiece is centered, tailstock force drives the chisel pins into the end face. The pins cut their grooves, and full torque is transmitted. The center point carries radial forces; the chisel pins handle circumferential torque – a perfect division of labor.
