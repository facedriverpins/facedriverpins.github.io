mechanical drive pins are the pins used in mechanical‑type face drivers – a design known for its simplicity, fast changeover, and excellent price‑to‑performance ratio.

A mechanical drive pin is the active component in a mechanical‑type face driver. Unlike pins in a hydraulic system , mechanical drive pins are fixed in the driver body but allowed to float axially through internal mechanical linkages – typically wedge levers or cross‑axis balancing plates. When tailstock force is applied, the centre point (which is axially movable in a mechanical driver) establishes the rotation axis, and the drive pins embed themselves into the workpiece end face. The wedge mechanism amplifies the clamping force and ensures that each pin engages independently, compensating for end‑face taper up to 5° or minor surface irregularities. In the world of end‑face drive systems, not every application requires a hydraulic unit. Many high‑production shops, gear cutters, and automotive suppliers rely on mechanical face drivers – robust, self‑contained systems that use wedge levers and balanced mechanisms to compensate for end‑face irregularities. And at the heart of every mechanical driver are the mechanical drive pins.

in a mechanical face driver, the centre point is movable while the pins are fixed in the driver body. The point first contacts the workpiece centre hole, aligns the part, and then, as tailstock force increases, the wedge mechanism forces the drive pins outward and into the end face. The pins and the point work as one integrated system: the point provides the precise rotation axis, and the pins transmit the torque. Neither can function properly without the other.
