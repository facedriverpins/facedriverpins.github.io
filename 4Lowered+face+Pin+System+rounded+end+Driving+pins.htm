Typical Lowered face driver pins applications include: Slim electric motor shafts, Precision gear mandrels, Small hydraulic spools, Thin-walled instrument shafts. Any workpiece with an end face diameter near or below the driver’s nominal minimum.

A Lowered drive pin is not defined by its physical height, but by its functional position. Unlike standard pins that rotate on a larger working circle, Lowered pins are positioned closer to the center axis of the face driver. This design reduces the effective working diameter of the pin set, allowing the driver to securely grip and drive workpieces that are significantly smaller than its rated minimum. In simple terms, Lowered pins lower the lower limit of what a face driver can machine.

When the part diameter falls below the driver’s nominal range, ordinary drive pins simply cannot reach the end face. You lose grip, torque transmission fails, and production stops. That is precisely why we specialize in Lowered drive pins – the solution for machining slim shafts, small arbors, and precision components that standard pins cannot handle.
