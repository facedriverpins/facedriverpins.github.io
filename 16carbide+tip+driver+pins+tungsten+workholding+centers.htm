Carbide tip drive pins widely used in automotive power train lines and bearing industry where part volumes are high and wear resistance directly impacts uptime.

Carbide tip drive pins are the standard choice for: Hard turning (hardened steels 50–67 HRC). Grinding operations (where stable, low‑impact forces allow carbide to excel). Abrasive materials (high‑silicon aluminum, chilled cast iron, metal matrix composites). Finish machining of gear shafts, camshafts, bearing races, and axle beams.

When workpiece hardness exceeds 48 HRC, standard tool steel drive pins fail quickly. Their edges wear, grip is lost, and production stops. For hardened steels, carbides, and abrasive materials, only one solution delivers the required life and reliability: Carbide Tip drive pins.

A Carbide Tip drive pin is a drive pin whose active cutting edge – the part that bites into the workpiece end face – is made of cemented carbide (typically tungsten carbide with a cobalt binder). Unlike solid carbide pins, our carbide tip design combines a tough steel body (often S7 tool steel) with a brazed or clamped carbide tip. This gives you the best of both worlds: the extreme hardness and wear resistance of carbide exactly where it is needed, and the toughness of steel in the pin body to withstand mounting stresses.

The center point (live or dead center) provides precise rotational axis alignment. Carbide tip drive pins work alongside that center. On hardened workpieces, we also recommend a carbide center point to prevent the center from wearing prematurely. Together, a carbide center plus carbide tip drive pins form a complete “all‑hard” clamping system for hard turning and grinding.
